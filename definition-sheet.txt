3. DENSITY DEFINITION — LOCKED, AND WHY
================================================================
Locked density: MARGINAL, GROUP-ALL WITH HIT-CONSISTENT
FALLBACK, SINKING-AWARE PLACEMENT-COUNT. In full:
- Score(square) = sum, over remaining (unsunk) ships, of the
  number of placements of that ship that:
  (a) cover the square,
  (b) avoid every miss cell,
  (c) avoid every sunk-ship cell,
  (d) when unsunk hits exist, cover ALL unsunk hits, IF any
      placement of that ship does so; otherwise, for that
      ship only, cover at least one unsunk hit (hit-
      consistent fallback).
  In practice: after a single hit, group-all and hit-
  consistent coincide. After two or more collinear hits
  from one ship, group-all restricts to placements that
  could be that ship — which is why it beats hit-
  consistent by 1.07 shots paired (section 9). When unsunk
  hits belong to two different ships, no single placement
  covers all of them, so the fallback applies ship by ship.
- Sunk lengths are REMOVED from the remaining-ship sum.
  Sunk cells are BLOCKED for all remaining ships.
  Miss cells are BLOCKED. Shot squares are never reshot.
- This is a PLACEMENT-COUNT, not a joint probability.
  It double-counts boards where two ships could cover the
  same square, and it does not enforce that different ships'
  placements are disjoint from each other. The script must
  say "placement-count" or "highest placement-count", never
  "most probable" for this score, unless the production
  engine upgrades to exact joint density (see section 9).
- Rejected alternative 1: marginal-IGNORE-hits (placements
  not filtered by hits, except never reshooting). Benchmark:
  56.94 shots vs 46.47 for hit-consistent on the same 300
  paired fleets — 10.46 shots worse, paired 95% CI +/-1.08.
  That WOULD put us behind BPK by about 12 shots, so it is
  REJECTED under Decoder's constraint, not locked.
- Superseded (not rejected for weakness): marginal
  HIT-CONSISTENT alone. Benchmark, same 300 paired fleets:
  hit-consistent mean 46.47, median 46.0; group-all mean
  45.40, median 45.0; paired group-all minus hit-consistent
  -1.07 shots, 95% CI +/-0.61. Group-all is therefore the
  locked definition, because Decoder's constraint requires
  the stronger option. Hit-consistent remains the documented
  fallback inside group-all (section 3, clause d), not a
  separate locked arm.
- Rejected alternative 2 (at benchmark stage, see section 9):
  exact joint density every shot at full tournament scale.
  Not rejected because it is weaker — it is stronger — but
  because it is not feasible at 10,000-fleet scale on a
  single CPU in days. It remains the upgrade path, not the
  locked scope, unless a compiled benchmark proves otherwise.
  Do not lock both group-all and joint at once: one density
  definition per production run, stated on screen.

4. TOURNAMENT ARMS — FOUR, NON-OVERLAPPING
================================================================
Metric for every arm: SINGLE-PLAYER shots to sink the whole
fleet, on the same paired fleets across arms (see section 8).
Do not run "parity + target" and "targeting-after-hit" as
two overlapping arms — they double-count targeting.

Arm A — Random (no targeting).
  Every shot uniform among unshot squares, ignoring hits.
  No reshooting. Analytic baseline, not a sampled estimate:
  expected shots to sink all 17 fleet cells = expected
  maximum position of 17 distinct cells in a random
  permutation of 100 = 17 x 101 / 18 = 95.39 shots.
  A sampled Random run is a check on that figure only.

Arm B — Parity hunt + Target.
  Hunt: only squares of one colour, (row + column) even.
  50 squares, in random permutation order per game (see
  section 7). Every ship is length 2 or more, so every ship
  covers at least one parity square.
  On a hit, switch to Target (section 5). When the target
  stack empties and a ship is sunk, return to parity hunt
  on remaining parity squares.

Arm C — Probability-density (LOCKED NAME IN SCRIPT:
  "placement-count" strategy, see section 3).
  Every shot = argmax placement-count score, recomputed
  after every shot. No separate target stack; targeting
  emerges from the score, because placements covering
  unsunk hits score highly near those hits.
  Tie-break: section 6.

Arm D — Hunt + Target (no parity, no density).
  Hunt: uniform random among unshot squares, random
  permutation per game.
  On a hit, switch to Target (section 5). On sunk, return
  to hunt.
  This arm isolates targeting value from parity value
  and from density value. Without it, no claim about how
  much of parity's gain is parity and how much is targeting
  is defensible.

Optional bonus arm, NOT part of the primary claim:
  Density hunt + explicit Target stack. Only if time
  permits. It muddies the density-vs-targeting comparison
  if included in the primary claim, so it must be labelled
  as a bonus, never as the primary density result.

================================================================
5. TARGET STACK — LOCKED VERBATIM (Arms B and D)
================================================================
1. On a hit, push its orthogonal (up, down, left, right)
   unshot neighbours onto a stack, in neighbour order:
   up, right, down, left. Stack is LIFO (last pushed pops
   first).
2. Pop and shoot. On a second hit in the same connected
   group, follow the line: extend in both directions along
   the hit axis to its ends, shooting unshot squares in
   line order, until the ship sinks or a miss bounds an end.
   Implementation in the benchmark: line extensions are
   pushed AFTER side neighbours, so they pop FIRST (LIFO).
3. On a miss while targeting, continue with the next stack
   entry. Do NOT return to hunt until the stack is empty.
4. Multiple hit groups: the most recent group is finished
   first, because its entries are on top of the stack.
   Groups are queued in hit order underneath, not
   interleaved, unless a new hit creates a new group on
   top of the stack.
5. On sunk: identify the sunk length from the size of the
   contiguous hit group (see section 6 for the assumption),
   remove one ship of that length from the remaining fleet,
   clear that group's hits from the unsunk set, resume hunt.
6. Never reshoot a miss, hit, or sunk cell. No diagonal
   targeting.

================================================================
6. SUNK ANNOUNCEMENT, TIE-BREAKING, PARITY COLOUR
================================================================
- Sunk announcement: YES, sunk ship identity is known in
  the sense that matters here — the sunk LENGTH is inferred
  from the size of the contiguous hit group at sinking.
  Alternative (only "a ship sunk, length unknown") is NOT
  locked, because it changes density and targeting
  materially. Sunk cells are blocked for other ships, and
  hits from a sunk ship are removed from the unsunk hit set.
  This matches standard play ("you sunk my battleship")
  and matches BPK's sinking-aware file names in its repo,
  though BPK's exact sunk handling was not read from its
  code through the tool path used here — so this is OUR
  locked assumption, stated as such, not a verified BPK fact.
- Tie-breaking for equal density scores (Arm C):
  among max-score squares, choose the smallest distance
  to board centre (4.5, 4.5), then lowest row-major index.
  Deterministic and reproducible. An unstated tie-break
  makes the result unreproducible, so this rule must be in
  the numbers log and, in short form, on screen.
  Centre-first is principled, not arbitrary: among equal
  marginal counts, centre squares are the ones joint
  probability would favour, and lowest-index tie-breaking
  alone would systematically bias shots to the top-left.
- Parity colour: (row + column) EVEN hunts first. The hunt
  colour does NOT switch after a sunk ship. Symmetric in
  expectation, but the code must pick one and record it.
- Random hunt order: Arms B and D hunt in a random
  permutation per game. Evaluation must sample hunt
  permutations as well as fleets, and report that.

================================================================
7. HUNT ORDER, SEEDING, PAIRING
================================================================
- Fleets are sampled by rejection from uniform random legal
  placements (section 1), seeded and recorded in the numbers
  log. The same fleets are used across all arms (paired
  comparison) to cut variance.
- Hunt permutations are seeded per game per arm and recorded
  by seed, not by listing every permutation.
- Production sample sizes are in section 8. Benchmark
  sample sizes (300 full-board fleets, 50 small-board
  paired fleets) are NOT production sizes — they are benchmarks.

================================================================
8. SAMPLE SIZES, CONFIDENCE INTERVALS, METRIC
================================================================
- Primary metric: single-player shots to sink, mean and
  median, with standard deviation and 95% confidence
  interval for the mean. Report the CI method in the log:
  mean +/- 1.96 x SD / sqrt(n) for the tournament arms,
  and paired differences (same fleets) for arm-vs-arm
  claims, because paired differences have smaller variance
  than unpaired differences.
- Production tournament: 10,000-20,000 sampled fleets,
  paired across arms. Rationale: shot-count SD is about
  8-13 shots (benchmark), so 10,000 fleets gives a mean
  95% CI half-width of about 0.2 shots, and a paired
  difference CI well under 1 shot — enough to separate
  arms that differ by tens of shots, and enough for the
  placement beat (section 10) where differences are about
  1 shot.
- If production time forces a smaller sample, the CI must
  widen in the script and on screen. Do not report a
  10,000-fleet CI on a 1,000-fleet run.
- Two-player win rate is NOT the primary metric. If any
  win-rate claim is added later, it needs a separate lock:
  first-player rule, both placement distributions, and
  alternating-shot order. Do not frame placement as a
  win rate on a single-player shots metric.

10. PLACEMENT SECOND BEAT — DEFINITIONS
================================================================
- Placement strategies must be defined as DISTRIBUTIONS
  with a sampling rule, or the beat is undefined.
  Locked examples (production may use these or lock
  equivalents, but must state them):
  P-random: uniform random legal placement (section 1).
  P-edge: uniform among placements satisfying a stated
    edge condition (e.g. all ships touch an edge, or at
    least a stated number do) — condition to be stated
    in the numbers log, not left as "edge-y".
  P-corner: uniform among placements satisfying a stated
    corner condition (e.g. at least one ship in a corner,
    or average distance from centre above a threshold).
  P-spread / P-cluster: uniform among placements
    satisfying a stated spread/cluster statistic.
  Implementation: rejection-sampling uniform placements
  until the condition holds, and report the condition and
  the strategy's placement count or its estimate.
- Opponent for the placement beat: PRIMARY = Arm C density
  (strongest, most realistic strong opponent, locked
  definition in section 3). SECONDARY = Arm B parity+target
  (human-like). Metric: average shots to sink,
  single-player, lower = better placement.
- What is exact vs sampled in this beat:
  Exact: one specific board vs a deterministic searcher
    (fixed tie-break) — about 45 marginal steps, cheap.
  Exact as a proxy, NOT as full-game shots: a placement's
    summed heatmap exposure (sum of heatmap scores over
    its cells) — do not present exposure as shots.
  Sampled, with CIs: a placement strategy's full-game
    average over all its placements. A strategy's placement
    set can be millions to billions, so it must be estimated
    over sampled placements from that strategy.
- Production placement sample: 10,000+ placements per
  strategy, paired fleets where possible. Rationale:
  placement differences are about 0.5-2 shots with SD
  about 10, so 10,000 per strategy gives a difference SE
  of roughly sqrt(2) x 10 / sqrt(10,000) = 0.14 shots —
  enough for a 1-shot difference, marginal for 0.5 shots.
  If a placement difference is smaller than its CI, the
  script must say "no measurable difference", not invent
  a winner. That is BPK's own conclusion for fancy
  placement, and matching it honestly is ahead of
  inventing a placement winner.
- BPK placement beat, for comparison: BPK reported
  placement recaps (Corners, Edges, Into Edges, Center
  percentages vs random) and concluded fancy placement
  barely beats random. Our placement beat is AT BPK if
  it uses the same board/fleet, the same strong opponent,
  and a sample large enough to separate a 1-shot effect.
  It is BEHIND BPK if it uses a smaller board, a weaker
  opponent, or a sample so small that a 1-shot effect
  cannot be separated. Do not lock a smaller placement
  sample to save time.

  REJECTED. Sampling fleets for evaluation is allowed and

  joint becomes the locked definition and the script
  wording changes to joint probability — that would be
