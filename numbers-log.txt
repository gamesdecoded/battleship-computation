Hunt permutations: per game per arm, seeded from base seed +

per strategy) and use sqrt(SE1^2 + SE2^2).
Metric everywhere: single-player shots to sink the whole
fleet. Lower shots = better SEARCH strategy. For PLACEMENT
(hiding), higher shots = fleet survives longer = better hiding.
See the flagged sheet wording correction below — do not invert
this in a script.

DEFINITIONS USED (locked, definition-sheet.txt)
================================================================
Board 10x10. Fleet, labelled, in order: Carrier 5, Battleship 4,
Cruiser 3, Submarine 3, Destroyer 2. Touching allowed.
Placements per labelled ship: 5: 120, 4: 140, 3: 160, 3: 160,
2: 180. Total placement lists: 760.
Density: group-all marginal placement-count, sinking-aware.
Score = sum over remaining (unsunk) ships of placements
covering the square, avoiding all miss cells and sunk cells,
and, when unsunk hits exist, covering ALL unsunk hits if any
placement of that ship does so, otherwise covering at least
one unsunk hit for that ship. Sunk lengths removed.
Tie-break: smallest distance to board centre (4.5, 4.5), then
lowest row-major index. Deterministic.
Parity colour: (row + column) even, no switch after sinking.
Target stack (Arms B and D): LIFO, neighbours pushed in order
up, right, down, left; line extensions pushed after side
neighbours so they pop first; most recent group finished
first; miss continues to next stack entry; never reshoot;
no diagonal targeting.
Sunk announcement: sunk length known at sinking. In code, a
ship is sunk when all its cells are shot (standard sunk
announcement: defender names the sunk ship). See deviation
note below.

================================================================
FLAGGED DEVIATIONS / CORRECTIONS — DO NOT HIDE THESE
================================================================
1. Sunk detection wording (sheet section 6): the sheet phrases
   sunk length as "inferred from the size of the contiguous
   hit group". Code uses true-ship sunk detection (all cells
   of a ship shot). These coincide when a hit group is a
   single ship. When two ships touch and their hit cells
   merge into one connected group, group-size inference is
   ambiguous (group size != sunk ship length), so true-ship
   detection is used — the same method as the benchmark
   prototype that produced the benchmark numbers. This is a
   clarification of an unimplementable-as-written phrasing,
   not a change to board, fleet, density, stack or metric.
   If a future script claims group-size inference literally,
   it must be corrected to sunk-announcement wording.
2. Placement "better" wording (sheet section 10): the sheet
   says "average shots to sink, single-player, lower = better
   placement". That line is INVERTED for a hiding strategy
   and is NOT followed in this log. A hiding placement that
   forces the searcher to take MORE shots is better hiding:
   higher shots = better hiding, lower shots = worse hiding.
   Evidence in this run: interior (centre) placements take
   only 37.54 shots against density, edge placements take
   51.98 — centre is searched first, so centre hiding is
   worse, not better. Any script winner claim must use
   higher = better hiding, and the sheet line must be
   corrected before scripting. No winner claim below
   depends on the inverted line.
3. Placement strategies are the sheet's "locked examples or
   equivalents", stated exactly here (section: placement
   strategies). They are not BPK's placement definitions;
   BPK's placement definitions were not read from its code.
4. BPK comparison is UNPAIRED level comparison only. BPK's
   fleets, seeds and exact density formula are not public
   in the material read here. No paired difference vs BPK
   is claimed.

PLACEMENT BEAT — PRODUCTION (10,000 placements per strategy)
================================================================
Strategies, stated exactly (rejection sampling from uniform
random legal placements until the condition holds):
  P-random: uniform random legal placement.
  P-edge: EVERY ship touches at least one board edge
    (row 0/9 or column 0/9).
  P-corner: at least one ship occupies a corner cell
    (0,0), (0,9), (9,0) or (9,9).
  P-interior: NO ship touches any board edge (all ships
    fully interior).
  P-spread: NO two ships touch each other, even diagonally
    (Chebyshev distance >= 2 between cells of different ships).

Against PRIMARY opponent, Density group-all (strongest):
  P-random:   mean 45.11, median 45.0, SD 8.76, CI +/-0.17
  P-edge:     mean 51.98, median 52.0, SD 7.67, CI +/-0.15
  P-corner:   mean 49.15, median 49.0, SD 8.18, CI +/-0.16
  P-interior: mean 37.54, median 36.0, SD 9.09, CI +/-0.18
  P-spread:   mean 46.45, median 46.0, SD 8.02, CI +/-0.16
  Unpaired differences vs P-random, density opponent
  (higher = better hiding, see correction above):
    P-edge:     +6.87, CI +/-0.23  [ +6.64, +7.10 ]
    P-corner:   +4.05, CI +/-0.23  [ +3.81, +4.28 ]
    P-spread:   +1.35, CI +/-0.23  [ +1.11, +1.58 ]
    P-interior: -7.57, CI +/-0.25  [ -7.81, -7.32 ]
  All four differences are separated from zero at 10,000
  samples. Against density, edge hiding is best, interior
  (centre) hiding is worst by a wide margin.

Against SECONDARY opponent, Parity+Target (human-like):
  P-random:   mean 61.28, median 62.0, SD 8.70, CI +/-0.17
  P-edge:     mean 60.98, median 62.0, SD 8.30, CI +/-0.16
  P-corner:   mean 61.29, median 62.0, SD 8.32, CI +/-0.16
  P-interior: mean 59.52, median 60.0, SD 9.61, CI +/-0.19
  P-spread:   mean 67.57, median 68.0, SD 5.73, CI +/-0.11
  Unpaired differences vs P-random, parity opponent:
    P-edge:     -0.30, CI +/-0.24  [ -0.54, -0.06 ]
      (statistically non-zero but tiny; in plain language:
      essentially no difference)
    P-corner:   +0.01, CI +/-0.24  [ -0.22, +0.25 ]
      (NO measurable difference — do not claim a corner
      advantage against parity)
    P-interior: -1.76, CI +/-0.25  [ -2.01, -1.51 ]
    P-spread:   +6.29, CI +/-0.20  [ +6.08, +6.49 ]
  Against parity, spread hiding is best by a wide margin;
  edge and corner are essentially a wash.

Paired opponent differences, same placement fleets,
density minus parity (negative = density sinks faster,
as expected for the stronger searcher):
  P-random:   -16.17, CI +/-0.24
  P-edge:     -9.00,  CI +/-0.23
  P-corner:   -12.14, CI +/-0.23
  P-interior: -21.98, CI +/-0.26
  P-spread:   -21.11, CI +/-0.20

Placement conclusion, honest and opponent-dependent:
  There is NO single placement strategy that wins against
  both opponents by a large margin. Against a density
  searcher, hide on the edges (best: +6.87 vs random).
  Against a parity human-like searcher, spread ships
  apart so one hit does not lead to another (best: +6.29
  vs random; edge is a wash there, corner is no
  difference there). A script must not claim "edge is
  the best hiding" without naming the opponent, and must
  not claim a corner advantage against parity.
  Cross-check: P-random vs density (45.11) matches the
  tournament density result (45.17) on different fleets,
  as it must — same searcher, same fleet distribution.
