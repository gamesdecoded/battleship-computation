ADVANCED-BEYOND-BPK INVESTIGATION — BATTLESHIP COMPUTATION TEST
humandecoded, Battleship computation test
Date of runs: 2026-10-29 (PKT, evening)
Status: BONUS / ADVANCED arms only. These results do NOT replace the locked
primary production numbers in results.json and numbers-log.txt.
Primary production (locked, 20,000 paired fleets, seed 20291029):
  Density group-all placement-count: 45.17 mean, 45.0 median, 95% CI +-0.12.
  Parity+Target (human-usable rule): 61.34 +-0.12.
  Hunt+Target: 66.36 +-0.18. Random analytic baseline: 95.39 shots.
BPK comparison throughout is UNPAIRED and flagged: BPK's fleets, seeds and exact
density formula are not public, and BPK's figures are rounded with no CI
(about 45 for density, about 63 for hunt-target). No "beats BPK" claim is made
on an unpaired level comparison alone.

METHOD
- Starting point: production engine engine.py (same definitions as
  definition-sheet.txt: 10x10, labelled fleet 5-4-3-3-2, touching allowed,
  uniform random legal placements, group-all marginal placement-count density,
  sinking-aware, parity colour (row+col) even, target stack LIFO neighbours
  up/right/down/left with line extensions pushed after side neighbours so they
  pop first, tie-break centre distance then row-major index).
- Advanced search arms: 3,000 paired fleets, fresh seed 20291101 (distinct from
  production seed 20291029 and benchmark seed 20261029), same fleets across all
  advanced search arms, so paired differences are valid within this sample.
  On these 3,000 fleets the density reference itself is 44.90 mean, 44.0 median,
  CI +-0.31 — slightly below the production 45.17 because it is a different
  3,000-fleet sample. Paired differences below are vs this 3,000-fleet density
  reference, which is the honest comparison; absolute means are also given.
- Placement x search game: 2,000 placements per strategy, same placements
  across searchers within each strategy (paired across searchers, unpaired
  across strategies). Mix runs: 2,000 placements, alternating strategies.
- CI method: mean +- 1.96 x SD / sqrt(n); paired differences use the same
  fleets. "Material" means a CI-separated gain of at least several shots, not
  a fraction of a shot — a 0.3-0.6 shot gain on 45 shots is real but not a
  headline advance.
- Sunk detection in all advanced arms: true-ship sunk announcement, as in
  production (see production deviation 1 — the definition sheet's
  "inferred from group size" phrasing must be corrected to sunk-announcement
  wording before scripting).

========================================================================
CANDIDATE 1 — HYBRID: DENSITY HUNT + EXPLICIT TARGET STACK
========================================================================
Idea: use density to find the first hit, then switch to the locked target
stack instead of letting density keep choosing every shot, with parity
switching after the smallest ship is sunk (spacing to every 3rd square when
only 3+ length ships remain, i.e. the mod-3 pattern).
Result, 3,000 paired fleets:
  Density (reference):        44.90 mean, 44.0 median, CI +-0.31
  Hybrid density+stack:       56.63 mean, 57.0 median, CI +-0.28
    paired diff vs density:   +11.73 shots, CI +-0.35  (WORSE)
  Hybrid+mod3:                56.92 mean, 57.0 median, CI +-0.28
    paired diff vs density:   +12.02 shots, CI +-0.36  (WORSE)
Grade: BEHIND BPK / BEHIND density. Dead end.
Why it fails (measured, not guessed): density's advantage is not only in the
hunt. After a hit, density re-scores every square with the hit information and
skips neighbours that cannot belong to a remaining ship, while a dumb stack
spends shots probing all four neighbours and side branches. Replacing density
targeting with a stack throws away about 12 shots. This is consistent with
production: parity+target (61.34) and hunt+target (66.36) are far behind
density (45.17) even though their hunt is weaker too.
Script wording: do NOT present hybrid as an improvement. If mentioned at all,
it is a measured dead end: "we tried handing the hunt to the stack — it cost
12 shots, so the computer keeps choosing every shot."

========================================================================
CANDIDATE 2 — SHIP-LENGTH-AWARE DENSITY REFINEMENTS (each tested separately)
========================================================================
All on the same 3,000 paired fleets, diffs vs density 44.90 in this sample.

2a. Mod-3 density (no stack): density hunt restricted to squares with
    (row+col) divisible by 3 once only 3+ length ships remain, to guarantee
    hitting every remaining ship's segment.
    Mean 45.37, median 45.0, CI +-0.31; paired diff +0.47 +-0.14.
    Grade: BEHIND (small but CI-separated worse). Density already accounts
    for remaining lengths; forcing the spacing pattern wastes shots.
    Dead end as a headline refinement.

2b. Normalised density: per-ship score = placements of that ship covering the
    square divided by that ship's total valid placements, summed over
    remaining ships (each remaining ship gets equal weight, instead of longer
    ships dominating by placement count).
    Mean 44.62, median 44.0, CI +-0.31; paired diff -0.27 +-0.18.
    Grade: AT BPK, tiny gain. CI excludes zero, but 0.27 shots is 0.6% —
    not material, and not a headline. Keep as a footnote refinement only.

2c. Length-weighted density: placements weighted by ship length L.
    Mean 44.88, median 44.0, CI +-0.32; paired diff -0.01 +-0.14.
    Grade: AT BPK, no effect. Dead end — length weighting moves nothing
    because the marginal count already scales with length.

2d. Line-bonus density: after two or more collinear unsunk hits, add a large
    bonus to the two line-extension squares (the squares continuing the line
    at either end), so density chooses the line continuation first, while
    still ranking extensions by placement count. This integrates line
    following INTO density scoring, not as a separate stack.
    Mean 44.33, median 44.0, CI +-0.31; paired diff -0.57 +-0.13.
    Grade: AT BPK, small but real. This is the best search refinement found:
    about 0.6 shots, about 1.3%, CI-separated. Against BPK's rounded about 45
    (unpaired, flagged) it is 0.67 shots below, but BPK's figure has no CI
    and is rounded, so this CANNOT be claimed as "beats BPK". It is a
    refinement of density, not a new strategy class.

2e. Normalised + line-bonus combined:
    Mean 44.43, median 44.0, CI +-0.32; paired diff -0.47 +-0.19.
    Grade: AT BPK, gains do not stack. Line-bonus alone (2d) remains best.
    Do not present the combination as better than line-bonus alone.

Overall candidate 2 verdict: NO search refinement beats BPK on shots by a
material margin. The best is line-bonus at about half a shot. That is worth
one honest sentence in a script, not a headline claim.

========================================================================
CANDIDATE 3 — PLACEMENT x SEARCH GAME (the counter-strategy loop)
========================================================================
BPK did placement as a one-sided recap. Production already showed placement
is opponent-dependent (10,000 per strategy): vs density, edge +6.87 (best),
corner +4.05, spread +1.35, interior -7.57 (worst); vs parity, spread +6.29
(best), edge -0.30 (wash), corner +0.01 (no difference), interior -1.76.
This investigation extends that to a counter loop, 2,000 placements per
strategy, same placements across searchers. Searchers: density, parity+target,
hunt+target, edge-aware density (density restricted per ship to edge-touching
placements only — the searcher that knows the hider hides on edges),
centre-first+target (human-model sensitivity, see candidate 4).

Table: mean shots to sink (higher = better hiding). CI about +-0.2 to +-0.6.
  Placement   vs density  vs parity  vs hunt  vs edge-aware  vs centre-first
  P-random        44.92      61.60    66.02        72.23           73.42
  P-edge          52.02      60.96    66.34        32.97           85.03
  P-corner        49.32      61.58    66.64        71.34           83.13
  P-interior      37.67      59.64    62.81        76.25           54.69
  P-spread        46.27      67.55    74.26        70.65           82.09
These reproduce production's pattern on a fresh sample (edge vs density
52.02 here vs 51.98 in production; spread vs parity 67.55 vs 67.57), which
validates the game table.

The counter loop, measured:
  Step 1 — hider plays edge (best vs unaware density: 52.02).
  Step 2 — seeker counters with edge-aware density: edge collapses to 32.97,
           a 19.05-shot reversal, far BELOW random (44.92). The edge rule,
           once known, is worse than hiding at random.
  Step 3 — hider's best response among tested pure strategies against the
           tested searcher set is then corner: its worst case across
           density/parity/hunt/edge-aware is density at 49.32 (edge-aware
           does not hurt corner, 71.34, because corner fleets are mostly not
           all-edge). But a corner-aware searcher was NOT tested, and by the
           same logic it would be expected to cut corner too. The loop does
           NOT converge to a pure strategy in the tested set.
  Step 4 — mixing hiding rules improves the worst case within the tested
           searcher set (2,000 placements, alternating strategies):
             edge/corner 50/50: vs density 50.72 +-0.35, vs edge-aware
               52.44 +-0.94, vs parity 61.22 +-0.36. Worst case 50.72 —
               better than pure corner's 49.32 and pure random's 44.92.
             edge/spread 50/50: worst case 49.15 (density).
             corner/spread 50/50: worst case 47.65 (density).
           The edge/corner mix is the best maximin measured. BUT a bespoke
           mixed counter-searcher (e.g. mixing edge-aware and corner-aware
           searchers) was NOT tested, so this is NOT a proven equilibrium.
           Do NOT claim "unexploitable placement" or "equilibrium proven".
           The honest claim is weaker and still real: mixing rules raises the
           worst case against every counter-searcher we tested, while every
           single rule we tested has a counter that kills it.

Grade: ADVANCED BEYOND BPK — not on shots, but on scope. BPK did not show
this loop. The game itself is the advance: a one-sided placement recap
becomes a two-player loop with a measured counter, a measured collapse, and
a measured mixed-hiding answer. This is the strongest scriptable advanced
angle in this investigation.
Honest wording (use this shape, not a stronger one):
  "Every hiding rule has a counter-search, and we measured the counter.
   Hide on the edges and the computer finds you in 33 shots once it knows.
   The best defence we measured is not one rule — it is randomising
   between two rules, which holds about 51 shots against every searcher
   we tested."
Do NOT say: "we found the unexploitable placement" / "the equilibrium" /
"the perfect hiding strategy". Those are not measured.

========================================================================
CANDIDATE 4 — HUMAN PLACEMENT / HUMAN SEARCH MODEL
========================================================================
Sources searched (web-guide searches, 2026-10-29): no citable, clean human
placement-frequency dataset was found. What exists is weak and must not be
presented as data:
  - A general visual-attention claim (eyes about 3x more likely to gravitate
    to the upper middle) — not a Battleship dataset; applying it to fleets
    is inference, not measurement.
  - A blog-style citation of an unnamed cognitive scientist (players avoid
    centre and corners) — no verifiable dataset.
  - Anecdotal tips (columns 1, 5, 10 most guessed; hard-to-say coordinates
    best hiding) — anecdote, not data.
  - One small real-games collection (83 two-person games; heat maps favouring
    rows B and I, columns 2, 4, 9; size-2 ships often in corners) — single
    group, tiny sample, not a general frequency table.
  - One survey (600+ online submissions after a Reddit link; corners
    populated about a quarter of the time in two corners) — the author
    himself flagged flaws: possible board flipping, duplicates not
    eliminated, click-to-rotate interface bias, respondents possibly
    messing with results. Do NOT use as a clean dataset.
  - One survey-style source (most popular I row and 9 column, C9/I8/I9,
    carrier more likely in middle regions) — sample size and source
    quality not established from the snippet. Do NOT treat as verified.
Grade: UNPROVEN as measured human data. Do NOT invent human frequencies.

Stated sensitivity model instead (explicit assumptions, labelled as model):
  Centre-first search: hunt squares in centre-distance order, then the
  locked target stack after a hit. This is a MODEL of a centre-biased human,
  not a measured human.
  On random fleets, 3,000 paired fleets: centre-first+target 72.93 mean,
  75.0 median, CI +-0.48; paired diff vs density +28.03 +-0.47. A
  centre-biased searcher is about 28 shots behind the computer.
  Against placements (2,000 per strategy, game table above), centre-first
  is worst against edge (85.03) and corner (83.13) and spread (82.09),
  and best (relatively) against interior (54.69). So UNDER THIS MODEL the
  answer flips: hide on the edges, not the centre — the opposite of the
  naive "hide where they look first" intuition, because a centre-first
  searcher spends its early shots in the centre and reaches the edges last.
  That flip is scriptable ONLY with the model label attached:
  "If your opponent searches from the centre out — a stated model, not a
  measured habit — then edge hiding survives about 85 shots in our model."
  It must not be presented as "humans search centre-first" as a fact.

========================================================================
CANDIDATE 5 — JOINT EXACT DENSITY ON A LIMITED, HONEST SCOPE
========================================================================
Joint density = probability a square is occupied given all hits/misses,
counting whole fleets jointly, not per-ship marginals. Small-board proxy
from feasibility (7x7, fleet [4,3,2], 205,692 labelled fleets enumerated,
50 paired fleets): joint exact 22.42 mean, marginal group-all 21.86 mean,
paired diff marginal-joint -0.56, CI +-1.17 — statistically indistinguishable
from zero, point estimate slightly in marginal's favour. On the only board
where joint can be measured exactly, joint does NOT beat marginal.
At full 10x10 scale, joint is infeasible as a tournament strategy: one
component of one joint candidate count took 1.44 seconds in compiled C
(232,207,034 completions); a full candidate is of order tens of seconds, one
joint shot of order tens of minutes, one game of order tens of hours (all
extrapolation from the measured component, stated as extrapolation, not
measurement). A 10,000-fleet joint tournament is out of the question, and
even a small-sample joint-vs-marginal paired test at 10x10 would need a
fundamentally smarter counting method proven by prototype first.
Grade: UNPROVEN as a gain, INFEASIBLE at scale. Dead end as a tournament
upgrade. Do NOT claim joint as an advance unless a future paired test shows
a CI-separated gain — the only paired evidence available shows no gain.

========================================================================
FINAL RECOMMENDATION
========================================================================
NONE of the tested candidates beats BPK on shots by a material margin.
Say that plainly:
  - Best search refinement (line-bonus density): 44.33 on 3,000 fleets,
    -0.57 +-0.13 vs density in the same sample — real, small, not a headline.
  - Hybrid density+stack: 11.73 shots WORSE. Mod-3 density: 0.47 worse.
    Length weighting: no effect. Joint: no measured gain, infeasible.
  - Density itself remains AT BPK (production 45.17 vs BPK about 45,
    unpaired, BPK rounded with no CI).

The strongest honest "advanced than BPK" angle is NOT a shots win. It is
candidate 3, the placement x search game, with candidate 2d as a supporting
refinement and candidate 4 as a labelled sensitivity beat:
  1. Keep the locked primary tournament and placement beat unchanged.
  2. Add the game beat: edge hiding -> edge-aware counter-search (52.02 to
     32.97) -> mixed edge/corner hiding (worst case 50.72 vs tested
     searchers) -> honest limit (mixed counter-searcher not tested, no
     equilibrium claimed).
  3. Add one line-bonus sentence: following the line after two hits in a
     row saves about half a shot over plain density in our paired test.
  4. Add the centre-model beat ONLY with the model label: if the opponent
     searches centre-first (model, not measured habit), edge hiding
     survives about 85 shots.
Recommended headline wording for the advanced claim (do not strengthen):
  "BPK showed you the best guess. We played the whole game — every hiding
   rule, its counter-search, and what survives when the opponent knows
   your rule."
Recommended wording that must NOT be used:
  "Our strategy beats BPK" / "faster than BPK's 45" / "the unexploitable
   placement" / "the perfect hiding strategy" / "humans hide ships here"
   (as a measured fact).

What remains unverified / not done:
  - A corner-aware searcher and a mixed counter-searcher were not tested;
    the equilibrium claim is therefore unproven, not merely unmeasured.
  - Joint density at 10x10 was not run as a paired game test (infeasible
    without a smarter counting prototype); the only joint evidence is the
    small-board proxy.
  - No clean human placement-frequency dataset was found; candidate 4
    stays a stated model.
  - All advanced search arms are on 3,000 fleets (not production's 20,000);
    their paired differences are CI-separated where claimed, but absolute
    means will shift slightly on a larger sample. If line-bonus is to be
    quoted in a script, re-run it on the production fleet set or quote the
    paired difference (-0.57) rather than the absolute 44.33.
  - Primary numbers in results.json / numbers-log.txt were not changed.
    Advanced arms are bonus only and must stay labelled as such in any
    script, so the audience can tell the tournament from the extras.
