METHOD
- Round-2 core arms: 3,000 paired fleets, fresh seed 20291102 (distinct
  from production 20291029, benchmark 20261029, round-1 20291101), same
  fleets across computer and human arms in advanced2.py, so paired
  differences within that sample are valid. On these 3,000 fleets the
  density reference is 45.29 mean (vs production 45.17 on 20,000 fleets —
  a different sample, so paired differences are the honest comparison).
- CI method: mean +/- 1.96 x SD / sqrt(n); paired differences use the
  same fleets. "Material" = several shots, CI-separated.
- Rollout: 100 fleets (first 100 of the same 3,000-fleet set, so paired
  with density on that subset), first 10 shots only, top-3 density
  squares, M=6 sampled fleets consistent with shots so far, density
  continuation to completion. Sampling is approximate (per-ship
  sequential, hit-guided; not an exact posterior) — flagged, not hidden.
- DP joint: row-by-row profile DP prototype, timed on realistic
  mid-game states captured from a density game. One DP count, not a
  tournament.
- Placement vs best: 2,000 placements per strategy, seed 20291103,
  same strategies as production (P-random, P-edge, P-corner,
  P-interior, P-spread, definitions in engine.py / definition sheet).
  Strategy-vs-strategy differences are UNPAIRED (different fleets per
  strategy); CIs below for those are unpaired.
- Sunk detection throughout: true-ship sunk announcement, as in
  production (see production deviation 1).

1. BEST COMPUTER SEARCH — judged only by paired shots to sink vs density
========================================================================
All on the same 3,000 paired fleets (density reference 45.29 in sample):

  Density group-all (reference):  45.29 mean, 45.0 median, CI +/-0.32
  Line-bonus density:             44.79 mean, 44.0 median, CI +/-0.32
    paired diff vs density:       -0.50 +/-0.12  (small, real, NOT material)
  Entropy / information-gain:     74.70 mean, 80.0 median, CI +/-0.71
    paired diff vs density:       +29.41 +/-0.70  (MUCH WORSE)
  Blend density+entropy:          45.42 mean
    paired diff vs density:       +0.13 +/-0.21  (no gain)
  Blend entropy+line-bonus:       45.33 mean
    paired diff vs density:       +0.05 +/-0.20  (no gain)

Line-bonus definition (same as round 1, re-measured here): after two or
more collinear unsunk hits, the two line-extension squares are forced
first, ranked by placement count among themselves. It is a refinement
of density, not a new strategy class.

Rollout / lookahead (first 10 shots only, top-3, M=6, 100 fleets):
  Density on same 100 fleets:     45.29 mean (see rollout100.json)
  Rollout first-10:               46.69 mean, 46.0 median, CI +/-1.83
    paired diff vs density:       +1.40 +/-1.90  (CI includes zero,
                                  point estimate WORSE)
  Cost: about 1 second per game for first-10 rollout (vs about 0.003
  seconds per density game). A full-game rollout at this cost would be
  about 4-5 seconds per game, and the first-10 version already shows
  no gain, so scaling it up is not justified on this evidence.
  Two implementation lessons, recorded honestly:
  (a) A sampled fleet whose remaining ship is already fully shot
      (all its cells are hits) makes density continuation loop forever,
      because that ship can never be sunk by a new shot. The sampler
      now rejects such fleets, and density continuation has a 100-shot
      safety cap. The first rollout runs that stalled were this bug,
      not a slow computer — the bug was fixed, not hidden.
  (b) Rollout sampling is approximate, not exact posterior. A future
      exact-posterior rollout could in principle differ, but it would
      need to beat this implementation by more than 1.4 shots just to
      break even, and by several shots to be material — no evidence
      for that is in hand.

DP joint counting (profile DP, one count):
  Mid-game stop-10 (4 ships remaining, 6 misses, 1 unsunk hit, 3 sunk
    cells): count 150,253,569 consistent fleets, 261,541 DP states,
    13.72 seconds for ONE count.
  Mid-game stop-20 (3 ships remaining, 14 misses, 1 unsunk hit, 5 sunk
    cells): count 651,896 consistent fleets, 8,363 states, 0.31 seconds
    for ONE count.
  Empty board (all 5 ships, no shots): NOT finished in over 120 seconds
    (state explosion, killed, not extrapolated as a measurement).
  Verdict: a joint shot needs about 100 such counts (one per candidate
  square, to get occupancy marginals). Even at the favourable 0.31
  seconds per count that is 31 seconds per shot and about 23 minutes
  per game; at the stop-10 13.72 seconds per count it is 22 minutes
  per shot. Joint is INFEASIBLE as a per-shot strategy on this DP,
  and the only paired joint evidence (small-board proxy, round 1:
  joint 22.42 vs marginal 21.86, diff -0.56 +/-1.17) shows no gain.
  Do NOT claim joint as a better core answer.

COMPUTER VERDICT: NO material computer improvement was found.
Best computer search = line-bonus density at 44.79 on 3,000 fleets,
-0.50 vs greedy in the same sample. That is essentially AT BPK's
reported 44.7 (unpaired, rounded, no CI), not materially below 45.
Greedy density is not proven optimal in general — most-likely-next-
square is not the same as fewest-total-shots — but none of the
tested non-greedy optimisers (rollout, entropy, blend, joint) beat
it by a material margin in this round. Say that plainly; do not
relabel a 0.5-shot refinement as a new best strategy.

2. BEST HUMAN-USABLE RULE — a rule a human can play by hand, no table
========================================================================
Human baseline (production, reproduced on the round-2 sample):
  Parity+Target (random parity hunt, locked target stack):
    61.48 mean, 63.0 median, CI +/-0.31 on 3,000 fleets
    (production 61.34 on 20,000 fleets; same rule, different sample)
    Paired gap to density in this sample: +16.20 +/-0.45

New human arms, same 3,000 fleets (all paired vs density in sample):
  Parity+Target, clear-adjacent on sink only:
    57.66 mean, paired vs density +12.37 +/-0.48
    (clear-adjacent = when a ship sinks, drop stack entries that
    touch the sunk ship; keep neighbours of other unsunk hit groups.
    This alone is worth about 3.8 shots vs the locked stack, which
    kept probing around a sunk ship.)
  Centre-parity + clear-adjacent:
    56.77 mean, paired vs density +11.48 +/-0.40
  Spacing-random + clear-adjacent:
    57.41 mean, paired vs density +12.12 +/-0.49
    (spacing = hunt squares (row+col) % m == 0, where m = smallest
    remaining ship length; m grows as ships sink, so the hunt board
    widens from checkerboard to every-third to every-fourth square.)
  Spacing-random + clear-adjacent + line-only:
    55.52 mean, paired vs density +10.23 +/-0.53
    (line-only = after two hits in a line, shoot only the two line
    extensions, ignore side neighbours until the ship sinks.)
  Spacing-centre + clear-adjacent + line-only:
    55.39 mean, paired vs density +10.10 +/-0.44
  Centre-parity + clear-adjacent + line-only  [BEST HUMAN]:
    54.61 mean, 54.0 median, CI +/-0.36
    paired vs density +9.32 +/-0.41
    vs baseline parity+target: 54.61 - 61.48 = -6.87 shots
    (conservative unpaired CI on that difference: +/-0.48; the
    fleets are the same, hunt randomness differs for the random arm,
    so the paired CI would be tighter, not looser)

Component checks (same sample, to show what each piece is worth):
  Centre-parity + clear-all + line-only: 55.20 (clear-all is 0.59
    worse than clear-adjacent — keeping other groups' neighbours
    matters).
  Centre-parity + clear-none + line-only: 57.28 (without clearing,
    line-only alone is worth about 4.2 vs baseline, but clearing is
    worth more).
  Centre-parity + clear-adjacent, no line-only: 56.77 (line-only is
    worth 2.16 on top of centre+clear: 56.77 -> 54.61).

BEST HUMAN RULE, in plain playable steps (centre version, 54.61):
  1. Hunt on black squares only — the checkerboard, (row+col) even.
     Start in the centre and work outward. (A printed centre-outward
     checkerboard diagram is the honest human form of this step; it
     is not a table you recompute each shot.)
  2. On a hit, shoot its neighbours.
  3. When you have two hits in a line, shoot ONLY the two ends of
     the line. Ignore the side neighbours until that ship sinks.
  4. When a ship sinks, stop shooting around it. Drop every target
     square that touches the sunk ship, and go back to hunting.
     Keep targets that belong to a different, still-unsunk hit.
  That is it. No density table, no computer, no recomputation.

Most memory-friendly human rule (no centre diagram needed):
  Spacing + clear-adjacent + line-only, 55.52 mean:
  1. Hunt on the checkerboard.
  2. Every time the smallest remaining ship sinks, widen the hunt:
     after the 2-ship sinks, hunt every third square; after the
     3-ships sink, every fourth square. (Every remaining ship must
     cover one such square, so you cannot miss a ship by widening.)
  3-4. Same line-only and drop-around-sunk rules as above.
  Cost of memorability: 55.52 - 54.61 = +0.91 shots (paired
  -0.92 +/-0.53 for centre-best minus spacing-line-only, i.e. the
  centre version is better by about 0.9, CI-separated but small).
  Present the centre version as best, and the spacing version as
  the version you can play from memory — do not present them as
  the same rule.

HUMAN VERDICT: MATERIAL improvement, and the strongest core win in
this round. The human gap to the computer falls from 16.20 shots
(61.48 vs density 45.29 in sample) to 9.32 shots (54.61 vs 45.29).
The human rule does NOT reach the computer (54.61 vs 45.29, still
9.3 behind), and must not be presented as if a human playing it
gets 45 shots. What it does is close almost half the gap — 6.9
shots — with four memorable steps. That is viewer-facing: it is
what people came to BPK to take away and use in a real game, and
it is better than BPK's human takeaway as measured here (BPK's
human rule in the material read was parity hunt/target, the
61.34 baseline; BPK's own checkerboard-greedy figure is a
computer rule, not a human rule, and must not be compared to
54.61 as if it were).

HIDING VERDICT: BEST hiding vs the best searcher is EDGE.
  - vs best computer (line-bonus): edge 50.75, best of all five
    strategies, +6.23 over random. The edge advantage shrinks vs
    line-bonus (from +7.09 vs plain density) because line-bonus
    finds edge fleets slightly faster, but edge still wins.
  - vs best human: edge 59.65, essentially tied with corner 59.41
    (difference +0.24, unpaired CI about +/-0.53 — not separated).
    Present edge and corner as tied at the top vs a human, not
    edge alone as a clear winner vs a human.
  - Worst case across the three searchers tested here (density,
    line-bonus, best human): edge 50.75, corner 48.33, spread 45.89,
    random 44.52, interior 37.52. Edge has the best worst case
    among pure strategies on this set.
  - Interior is the WORST hiding vs every searcher, and worst of
    all vs the best human (45.09, -9.70 vs random) — because a
    centre-first human hunts the interior first. Do not ever
    recommend interior hiding.
  - Limit, carried from round 1: edge collapses vs an edge-AWARE
    counter-searcher (round 1: edge-aware density 32.97, far below
    random). Edge is best vs the best searcher that does NOT know
    your rule; once the opponent knows you hide on edges, edge is
    worse than random. The hiding answer must state the opponent.
    The round-1 mixed edge/corner answer (worst case 50.72 vs
    density / edge-aware / parity) was NOT retested vs line-bonus
    or vs the best human in this round, so do not quote it as
    tested against the new best searcher.

4. WHAT WAS NOT TESTED / REMAINS UNPROVEN
========================================================================
- Exact-posterior rollout (vs the approximate sampler used here):
  untested. The approximate rollout shows no gain (+1.40 +/-1.90
  on 100 fleets, first 10 shots only); an exact version would need
  to beat that by several shots to be material, and would cost far
  more per game. Do not claim it as a future win, only as untested.
- Mixed counter-searchers vs the new best human / line-bonus:
  untested. No equilibrium claim is made in this round either.
- A joint tournament at 10x10: infeasible on the DP prototype
  (section 1). The DP count itself is exact (stop-10 count
  150,253,569 and stop-20 count 651,896 are exact counts for those
  states, given the blocked/hit inputs), but a per-shot joint score
  is not feasible, so joint is not a core answer.
- Rollout beyond the first 10 shots, rollout with larger M or k:
  untested. The first-10 scope was chosen for cost; a full-game
  rollout could differ, but the first-10 result gives no reason
  to expect a several-shot gain, and the cost is about 300x
  density per game.

5. LITERATURE (deprioritised, reported only where it bears on core)
========================================================================
Web search for optimal / expected-shots-minimising Battleship
strategies found no citable, paired, implementable algorithm that
beats greedy density by a material margin:
  - General guides (e.g. blinky.games strategy/history pages) claim
    density "about 40" and parity "about 50" — different models,
    and in one case medians mixed with means (DataGenetics medians
    must never be mixed with means; see the separate reference
    audit). Those figures are NOT a paired test vs our density
    and must not be cited as beating 45.17.
  - A POMDP framing (Battleship as a partially observable search,
    optimal policy = posterior belief over layouts, minimising
    expected shots to win) is the correct theory, but the sources
    found do not give an implementable policy that beats greedy
    density on 10x10 — they state the objective, not a better
    algorithm. Cited as theory, not as a tested improvement.
  - An older strafing / search-and-destroy algorithm (academia.edu
    paper) averaged 48.9 shots vs an opponent's 49.5 in 32 test
    games — worse than density 45.17, and on a tiny sample. Not
    a better core answer.
No literature-backed arm was therefore added beyond rollout,
entropy and DP-joint, which were the literature's own candidates
(lookahead, information gain, exact posterior) — and all three
were tested paired and did not beat greedy materially.
